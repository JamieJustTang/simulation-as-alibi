# Stream 3 — Governance Frameworks (6 frameworks)

As declared in the paper (Section 3.2, Data Streams): *"Stream 3 comprises six
governance frameworks: the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF 1.0, U.S. DoD AI
Acceleration Strategy, White House Executive Order on AI, EU Emergency
Management Framework for AI, and China's MOST AI Ethics Guidelines."*

## Diagnostic dimensions (Section 5, paper)

- **D1 — Interaction topology**: does the framework require disclosure of who
  may interact under what constraints?
- **D2 — Action grammar**: does the framework require disclosure of which
  actions are valid and who defined them?
- **D3 — Sanction architecture**: does the framework require disclosure of how
  rule violations are detected and enforced?
- **D4 — Beneficiary disclosure**: does the framework require a statement of
  whose interests the system's design serves?

## Notes

- The paper concludes that *none of the six contains an explicit
  architecture-level procedural authorship disclosure mechanism* (Section 5).
  NIST AI RMF 1.0 is marked as partial (△) on D1 only: its "Map" function
  could in principle encompass interaction architecture, but nothing in the
  RMF operationalizes this mapping as an explicit disclosure obligation.
- Full texts of the frameworks are protected by copyright and are not
  redistributed here; each is identified by name, version, and official
  source link.
- The paper's assessment (Table 3) records only whether each framework
  contains an explicit architecture-level procedural authorship disclosure
  requirement across dimensions D1–D4; it does not assess the frameworks'
  overall scope or quality.
